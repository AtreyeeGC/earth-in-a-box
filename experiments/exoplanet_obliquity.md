# Exoplanet Obliquity (Axial Tilt) Experiment

## Objective
Evaluate how varying planetary axial tilt ($\theta = 23.44^\circ, 45.0^\circ, 90.0^\circ$) alters spatio-temporal temperature distributions and seasonal extremes across a 1D grid.

## Key Findings
- **23.44° (Earth Baseline):** Equatorial region acts as the primary heat engine; North Pole seasonal range is 270.8 K – 282.5 K ($\Delta T = 11.7\text{ K}$).
- **45.0° (High Tilt):** High-latitude insolation increases during summer solstices, widening polar swings to 262.9 K – 283.8 K ($\Delta T = 20.9\text{ K}$).
- **90.0° (Sideways Planet):** Solstice insolation concentrates directly over the summer pole, driving extreme peak temperatures up to 329.7 K ($\Delta T = 32.7\text{ K}$). Equatorial regions experience their coldest temperatures during solstices.

## Astrobiological Implications
High-obliquity worlds experience extreme seasonal thermal stress, requiring either rapid heat-capacity damping (deep global oceans) or atmospheric heat transport to remain continuously habitable across all latitudes.
