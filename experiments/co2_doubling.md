# CO₂ Doubling Experiment

## Objective
Measure the model's Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS) by observing the dynamic thermal response to a doubling of atmospheric carbon dioxide.

## Setup
- **Baseline CO₂:** 280 ppm (Years 0–50)
- **Forced CO₂:** 560 ppm (Years 50–150)
- **Stellar Luminosity:** 1.00 (Sun-like)
- **Orbital Distance:** 1.0 AU
- **Surface Albedo:** 0.30

## Results

| Variable | Value |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pre-industrial Equilibrium (280 ppm) | 287.83 K |
| Doubled CO₂ Equilibrium (560 ppm) | 289.30 K |
| **Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ΔT)** | **+1.48 K** |

## Scientific Conclusion
Doubling atmospheric CO₂ lowers effective top-of-atmosphere emissivity, trapping outgoing infrared flux. The surface warms by +1.48 K to re-establish radiative equilibrium. Thermal inertia delays full relaxation, demonstrating climate system response time.
